# The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning (2007)

## What it is

An experiment in which college students learned to find the volume of four
solids, either one solid at a time (blocked) or with interleaving of all
four.

## Claims

- This source says interleaving lowered scores during the sessions and
  raised them a week later: the interleaving group solved roughly three
  times as many problems as the blocked group
  (supports:: [[interleaving]]).
- It says the benefit of interleaving is not the benefit of gaps between
  sessions: both groups had the same gaps.
- It reads interleaving as forcing the solver to pick the formula, which a
  blocked sheet does for them.
- It says textbooks rarely use interleaving, and counts it among
  desirable difficulties: desirable difficulties cost marks on the day and
  pay them back later (supports:: [[desirable-difficulties]]).

# Learning concepts and categories: is spacing the enemy of induction? (2008)

## What it is

An experiment in which participants learned the styles of twelve painters,
with each painter shown in a block or with interleaving of all twelve.

## Claims

- This source says interleaving the painters led to better recognition of
  new paintings by the same painters than blocking did
  (supports:: [[interleaving]]).
- It says most participants judged blocking to have worked better, even
  after doing better with interleaving.
- It places interleaving among desirable difficulties: desirable
  difficulties feel worse while learning and leave more behind, and the
  learner's own judgement cannot be trusted to find desirable difficulties
  (supports:: [[desirable-difficulties]]).
- It had expected the opposite result, since seeing one painter in a row
  should make the common style easier to see.

## Candidates

- Illusion of fluency - what feels easy while learning is taken for learning
